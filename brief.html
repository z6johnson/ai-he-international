NUD · Reading brief

Read these first.

Ten items, ordered for someone building fluency across all three fronts at once
01

Penprase & Pickus, The New Global Universities

Book · Princeton · 2023 · plus their LSE blog summary (Mar 2025)

The founding playbook. Eight detailed founding case studies — NYU Abu Dhabi, Minerva, Yale-NUS, Olin, Ashesi, Fulbright Vietnam, Ashoka, ALU — written by participants, not observers. Covers culture formation, curriculum design under founding conditions, and the quality-maximizing versus efficiency-seeking models. The single most NUD-relevant book on the list.

02

Helen Beetham, imperfect offerings

Newsletter · Substack · ongoing

The critical voice. The sharpest humanities-informed commentary on AI in higher education; coined the "AI fantasy and fatalism" framing. Essential precisely because it is skeptical without being reactionary.

03

Alex Usher, The World of Higher Education podcast and HESA blog

Newsletter + podcast · ongoing

The global strategic view, with recurring Gulf coverage. The most consistently global-minded daily commentary on higher education systems, funding, and policy — indispensable for the comparative view.

04

University of Sydney two-lane framework and Cogniti

Framework + platform · ongoing

The best worked example of assessment redesign anywhere: Lane 1 is secure, supervised assessment of learning; Lane 2 is open assessment where AI use is permitted and taught. Cogniti, their in-house platform for faculty-authored constrained AI agents, is the closest existing implementation of faculty authoring AI pedagogical behavior.

05

MBZUAI, read with the Moor Insights and Rest of World analyses

Case · Abu Dhabi · founded 2019

The Gulf anchor case: the first graduate research university dedicated entirely to AI, now with an undergraduate program. Read the institution alongside independent analysis — including the discrepancy in its own retention figures — as a working lesson in why Gulf institutional self-reporting needs cross-checking.

06

TEQSA assessment-reform resources

Regulator program · Australia · 2023–2025

The best regulator-led thinking. Argues for structural redesign over detection; its 2024 request for institutional AI action plans got a 100% response rate from all 203 providers and fed a toolkit drawing on 77 institutions.

07

The CAA versus KHDA/UQAIB accreditation regimes

Regulatory orientation · UAE

The operating terrain. Federal CAA recognition is what makes a degree valid for federal employment, professional licensing, and onward study across the UAE; Dubai free zones run a separate regime built for branch campuses. Which regime NUD enters — or whether it seeks both from the start — shapes who can enroll and whose degrees count where.

08

Michael D. Smith, The Abundant University

Book · MIT Press · 2023

The economics of what a university is. Argues the scarcity model in enrollment, instruction, and credentials is unsustainable — central to the credentialing question even though it predates the current wave.

09

Phil Hill, On EdTech

Newsletter + podcast · ongoing

The market, vendor, and institutional-politics view. The sharpest analyst of the higher-ed technology industry, LMS dynamics, and "AI sovereignty" — the best source for the vendor-dependence risk NUD has already named.

10

Dawson, Bearman, Dollinger & Boud, "Validity matters more than cheating"

Paper · Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education · 2024

The intellectual frame for the credential question: the field-shaping argument that assessment validity, not cheating prevention, should drive reform. The foundation for any institution planning witnessed human performance as its credential basis.

Caveats & standing risks

Built into every read of this dashboard
▲

The 12-month staleness rule

The field moves fast enough that any report older than about twelve months is historical context, not current state. The newsletters and podcasts matter more than one-off reports for exactly this reason — and the footer of this dashboard warns when the baseline itself goes stale.

▲

Much Gulf-facing material is promotional

Government and institutional sources are useful for intent and ambition; read them alongside independent analysts (Moor Insights, Rest of World, Al-Fanar, Al Qasimi Foundation, THE). MBZUAI's own retention figures — 80% in its Class of 2025 reporting versus up to 70% cited by its president — are the worked example. Some analyst notes are sponsored; flag them as such.

▲

The UAE regulatory picture is moving

The MoHESR free-zone licensing changes are recent. Verify current rules against CAA and KHDA primary sources before anything load-bearing goes into NUD advice.

▲

"AI-native" is unproven branding

The label is applied loosely, often as branding. No proven model exists yet, including among the institutions tracked here. That is a fact worth stating plainly in the engagement rather than papering over — NUD will face the same skepticism and should learn from how these institutions do or don't earn credibility.

▲

Two book entries carry dating caveats

Smith is 2023, Kosslyn & Nelson is 2017; both earn their place on substance, not recency. Read the Minerva account critically, alongside later independent assessments of how Minerva actually fared.
